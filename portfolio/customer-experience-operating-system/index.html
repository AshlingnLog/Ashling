The assignment behind the artifact

Sales training only becomes useful when it survives contact with the real operation.

The business was investing in sales-process training, but the model needed to reflect the actual work: how an urgent service request moved from first response through scoping, delivery, and follow-up.

I reconciled the training framework with the operating process and added two views that were easy to overlook: the person experiencing the service and the referral partner whose trust helped start the relationship.

The RevOps insight

I built this before I knew to call it revenue operations. The principle was already there: align people, process, and customer experience around one revenue journey.

Why this is revenue operations

RevOps is not a dashboard or software platform. It is the work of making the revenue journey coherent across teams.

One shared language

Sales training and operational reality became one usable model instead of competing versions.

Clearer handoffs

Each stage made the next responsibility visible, reducing gaps where trust and opportunities are lost.

Experience by design

The customer’s emotional needs were treated as part of execution—not something separate from the work.

Revenue beyond the sale

The process extended through delivery and follow-up because repeat referrals depend on the full experience.

My role

Turn the framework into something the team could use.

  • Reconciled that workflow with the company’s external sales training.
  • Added customer and referral-partner perspectives at every stage.
  • Identified the moments where specific behaviors build or weaken trust.
  • Designed a visual reference for training and cross-team alignment.
